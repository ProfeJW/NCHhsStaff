// Grading & Coursework

Gradebook Weights

Teachers are responsible for setting these weights in Progress Book.

45%Assessments
45%Classwork
10%Homework

Assessments = tests, quizzes, projects. Classwork = participation, labs, rubric-based project work.

Minimum one grade per week must be entered in Progress Book by Thursday.

Late Work & Retakes

  • Late work must be submitted within 2 weeks of the due date — except the last week of each quarter.
  • Deduct no more than 20% (two letter grades) for late work.
  • Retakes are non-negotiable at student request and must be reflected in the grade.
  • Retakes must occur within 2 weeks of the original assessment date.

Make-Up Work & Incompletes

  • Make-up work is the student’s responsibility — they must contact you immediately upon return.
  • For full credit, work should be completed within the number of days absent.
  • Suspended students may make up tests and assignments; work assigned before the suspension is accepted as previously assigned.
  • Incompletes must be made up within 2 weeks after the course ends. Fourth-quarter arrangements are made before the last day of school.

A student needs at least a “D” (1.0) average for the year to earn credit. Honor roll tiers: 4.0, 3.5, 3.0 — no grade below a C in the four major disciplines, and no F in any subject.

Homework Guidelines

  • Maximum daily outside preparation — 9th grade: 120 minutes; 10th–12th: 150 minutes. These are totals across all teachers, not per class.
  • Homework is not used for disciplinary purposes.
  • Assignments may be started in class to allow supervised study and questions.

Progress Reports, Plagiarism & Schedule Changes

  • Progress reports go out approximately every 5 weeks; dates are on the school calendar. Reports include reasons for lack of progress and recommended remedial action.
  • Plagiarism: the degree of penalty is at the discretion of the classroom teacher the work was submitted to. Appeals go to the building administrator after a parent/student/teacher conference.
  • Schedule changes require parent, counselor & administrator approval. A drop after the third week of class results in a grade of “F.”

// IEP, 504 & EL Supports
These are protected student records. IEPs, 504 plans and EL language plans are confidential under FERPA and IDEA. Access them through the Drive folder below — never copy goals or modifications into email, shared docs, printed sub folders, or anywhere outside staff-only systems.

Where the documents live

IEP / 504 & Language Plan folder — link coming

Requires an @nchcityschools.org sign-in. If a student on your roster isn’t in there, ask their intervention specialist — don’t assume there’s no plan.

Your responsibility

  • Read the plan for every student on your roster at the start of each term — and again whenever a plan is revised.
  • Implementing accommodations is not optional. They are a legal requirement, not a suggestion.
  • Document that you provided them — note accommodations on assessments and major assignments.
  • Keep printed plans out of sight of other students, and never leave them in a sub folder.
  • If you believe a plan isn’t working, contact the intervention specialist — do not change or drop an accommodation on your own.

Accommodations you’ll commonly see

  • Time & setting: extended time, small-group or separate testing, frequent breaks, preferential seating.
  • Presentation: read-aloud or text-to-speech, chunked directions, copies of notes, reduced items per page.
  • Response: scribe or speech-to-text, oral responses, calculator, word bank, graphic organizer.
  • Behavior: check-ins, cueing, movement breaks, a designated cool-down space.
  • EL language plans: bilingual dictionary or glossary, extended time, simplified or scaffolded language, translated directions.

This is a general orientation only. Always follow the individual plan — it governs, not this list.

// Classroom & Hallway Policies
Cell phones — Ohio House Bill 250: Students may have phones, but they must be turned off (not silent or vibrate) and left in their lockers during the school day. At no time should a student use a phone during the school day, or before, during, or after school instructional activities. All other electronic devices stay in lockers.

Enforcing the Phone Policy

  • This applies everywhere in the building — not just classrooms. A phone seen in the hallway, stairwell, restroom, or cafeteria counts the same as one seen in class.
  • A student seen with a phone anywhere may be sent to ACA for a consequence.
  • You do not have to confront the student. You may instead email Ms. JJ (Janeen Johnson, ext. 10022) about the prohibited use or possession — she will document it and issue the consequence.
  • Use whichever route fits the moment; the email option exists specifically to keep a phone from turning into a confrontation.

Hall Passes

  • Students complete an E Hall Pass and wear a lanyard pass around the neck to leave the room.
  • Passes are for emergencies only so students don’t miss instruction.
  • No passes during the first ten minutes or the last ten minutes of class.
  • No passes at all during CLD.
  • Students should use the restroom between periods.
  • Don’t write passes to the gym for students not in a scheduled PE/health class.
  • A student who feels ill needs an E Hall Pass and a written pass from you before reporting to the clinic.

Tardies & Arrival

  • Any student not in the classroom at the start of the period is tardy.
  • Students must be in first period no later than 8:15 AM and begin bell work immediately.
  • Students arriving after 8:15 enter through the front doors, clear security, and sign in at the swipe machine.
  • They have 5 minutes from their swipe to hit their locker and reach class.
  • A student who takes longer than 5 minutes to reach class after swiping in is sent directly to ACA.

Lockers

  • Students may visit their lockers at three times only: at the beginning of the day, at lunch, and at the end of the day.
  • Cell phones stay in lockers all day, along with all other electronics, book bags, and totes.
  • Locker trips outside those three windows are not a reason to write a pass.

Dress Code

  • Tops: a collared shirt (polo, button-up) in black, grey, white, red, light blue or navy blue. NCH spirit wear shirts are acceptable in place of a collared shirt.
  • Bottoms: black, khaki or navy skirts/skorts, shorts or pants. Length must be fingertips or longer. No sweatpants or jeans.
  • Leggings may be worn under dress code bottoms — solid, in an approved colour.
  • Sweatshirts: a solid white, red, blue, black or grey sweater, sweatshirt or lightweight fleece may go over the collared shirt. Logos no larger than a closed fist. Hoods are allowed on the garment but may not be worn on the head in the building. Jackets and coats are not permitted in the building.
  • Shoes: closed-toe required; athletic shoes required for PE. Not permitted: house shoes, backless shoes, crocs, flip-flops, bare feet, shoe skates, slides.
  • Head coverings are permitted only for verified medical or religious purposes. No scarves, bonnets, bandanas or head jewellery otherwise.
  • Any accessory that disrupts the learning environment is prohibited. Bags & backpacks stay in lockers; small purses/fanny packs up to 6×8″ may be carried.

// Attendance & Discipline

Attendance

  • Parents are expected to call in an absence by 9:00 AM.
  • A written or doctor’s note must reach the front office within a week of the absence or it stays unexcused.
  • Students must be in attendance each class that day to participate in after-school or extracurricular activities.
  • Students without current emergency medical forms in FinalForms are excluded from school beginning the first full week of September.

Alternative Classroom Assignment (ACA)

  • Runs 8:15 AM – 3:05 PM. Students sent out of class for disrupting the learning environment report to ACA.
  • The ACA monitor collects classwork from teachers for the duration. Students must complete it — failure to complete work results in an “F.”
  • Credit is granted if ACA is served as assigned, behavior is appropriate, and all work is completed to the best of the student’s ability as determined by the classroom teacher.
  • Labs, demonstrations, and group work can’t be duplicated in ACA; alternate assignments earn credit.

After-School Detention

  • Tuesday or Thursday, 3:15–5:15 PM. No student admitted after 3:15.
  • Students report to their locker and be in the assigned room by 3:15 PM.
  • Students bring enough work for the full period; no food, devices, or sleeping.

Emergency Removal — Teacher Rights

  • If a student is reinstated before an emergency-removal hearing, the teacher may request and must be given written reasons for the reinstatement.
  • The teacher cannot refuse to reinstate the student.
  • Suspended or ACA-placed students and a parent must meet with an administrator before returning to class.

// Emergency Procedures
Log your students in Naviance 360. During a fire drill — and any evacuation or lockdown — you must account for every student in the Naviance 360 app, not just on paper. Download it before the first drill so you aren’t setting it up in the parking lot. Naviance 360 download link — coming

Fire Drill

  • Close windows before leaving; last person out closes the door.
  • Walk rapidly in single file — do not run. No talking.
  • Once outside, take attendance and log every student in Naviance 360.
  • Report any student you cannot account for immediately.
  • Stay in line and quiet until all students are accounted for.

Tornado Drill

  • Move students to your room’s assigned area — routes differ by room and floor.
  • Protection position: crouch low on knees facing the wall, head down, arms covering the back of the head and neck. Under a desk or table in that position is better still.
  • Keep students clear of windows and doors.
  • Account for your students in Naviance 360 once in position.

// Reporting & Student Support
Mandatory reporting: All school personnel who become aware of harassment, intimidation, or bullying must immediately report it to the building principal. Any staff member receiving a report must put it in writing on the Board’s HIB complaint form and forward it to the principal.
  • Good-faith immunity: Staff who report promptly and in good faith under this policy are individually immune from civil liability.
  • Intervene promptly whenever you observe student-on-student misbehavior — even before it rises to the level of bullying.
  • Student/Parent Hotline (Speak Up!): 1-866-547-8362 (1-866-LISTEN2ME) — live attendant or voicemail.
  • 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline — call or text 988. Veterans press 1; en español marque 2 or text AYUDA.
  • Retaliation against anyone who reports or participates in an investigation is prohibited.

Texting Students — Staff Requirements

  • You must have written permission from the student and parent (if under 18) before texting, and must specify how and when you’ll use it.
  • All employees who text students must sign the District Texting Agreement.
  • Content must be school-related only — never a personal conversation. Use it sparingly.
  • If a student sends an inappropriate reply, contact the parent and administration immediately.
  • No expectation of privacy — district messages may be reviewed at any time.
